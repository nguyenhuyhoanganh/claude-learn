# Congratulations

Congratulations on reaching the end of Learn to Code with Rust. I had a blast recording these videos, and I hope you had a lot of fun watching the course. This course was in development for more than a year and a half, including a cancellation in the middle of production, so I'm happy to see the final product come to life. When I was learning Rust from the official Rust book, I found the language to be pretty complex and confusing, truth be told. In addition, I felt that the content's presentation tended to be more scientific, more academic, more dense and technical and low level. And so I had to supplement my learning by practicing in the code editor and working through tutorials and books and coding challenges to understand the big picture. The language had a lot of nuance and complexity, and I also felt that that hid some of the beauty in its design. It was just tough to grasp. And my hope in developing the course was to first demystify the content, but also make it a bit more friendly and fun, simpler and less academic. There's a lot of beautiful parts to Rust, and I know that I'll be applying the concepts that I learned here to my work in other languages. So what I love about Rust is I've grown as a developer and found new ways of thinking about code, not just in this language, but in other ones as well. And I got that all simply from building this course, and I hope you feel the same way from watching it. So whether you plan to use Rust in a professional setting or a personal one, I wish you all the best in your projects and endeavors, and I would love to see you in another course soon. So with that, I will bid you adieu and see you in the next one.
