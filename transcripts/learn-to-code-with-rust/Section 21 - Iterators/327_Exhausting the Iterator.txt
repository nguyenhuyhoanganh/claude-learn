# Exhausting the Iterator

Iterators in rust are lazy, which means they do not automatically start iteration when they are created. The iterator remains unactivated until another method is called or an operation occurs that begins the actual iteration process. It's just like the word lazy in the real world. Someone who is lazy doesn't want to do any work until they absolutely have to. Iterators. Being lazy means the same thing. They're not going to just start automatically. They will not initiate the iteration, the traversal, so to speak, until we explicitly tell them to do so. The process of completing that iteration is called using, consuming, or exhausting the iterator. The iterator can only be used once before it is exhausted, so iteration cannot occur again after the iterator has been exhausted. Exhausted just means that the iterator has yielded all of its elements advanced to its end, and there are no more items to be extracted from it.
